Blueprints: TV denoiser versus Gaussian denoiser (example)

An experiment for a lab whose CT reconstruction uses a Gaussian denoiser by default. This made-up example shows a front page while the design is under review and one pilot run is pending. One blueprint per entry. Start at the top and read down.

Design not yet settled: one pilot run is pending. Reviewer: to be decided before the design is settled.

The experiment

1. Goal

for review

Determine how much lower the total-variation (TV) denoiser's noise is than the Gaussian denoiser's at equal edge width, on scans of the Gammex MMC and Lungman phantoms, and whether muscle contrast, CT number accuracy, noise texture, or leakage between material maps gets worse.

2. Motivation

for review

Decide whether to change the lab's default from the Gaussian denoiser to TV. TV is about 80 times slower, so the gain must be large; the trade-offs are weighed and discussed rather than judged by one threshold.

3. Plan

for review

Reconstruct two scans of each phantom with both methods and score them. Choose TV's one weight on a third Gammex scan, by a staged search that matches TV's edge width to the Gaussian denoiser's at the muscle insert. About 17 GPU hours; a pilot run will measure TV's time per run.

4. Results

for review

Planned: for each quantity, a scatter plot of TV against the Gaussian denoiser with one point per slice, the mean and standard deviation of the paired differences or ratios, and example slices chosen by a fixed rule. Predicted: TV improves several quantities on the Gammex phantom, may show more leakage, and gains less on Lungman.
