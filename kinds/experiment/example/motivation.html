2. Motivation

What the lab will do differently depending on the answer. A made-up example; the numbers are illustrative.

1. Decision

The lab decides whether its default denoiser changes from the Gaussian denoiser to TV. The lab's members discuss the results, and the lab director decides.

2. What is known now

  • A reconstruction takes about 80 times longer with TV than with the Gaussian denoiser.
  • TV favors images made of regions of constant intensity, so it may gain more on phantoms than on patients.
  • TV's edge width depends on the edge's contrast; the Gaussian denoiser's does not.
  • TV may lower the contrast of low-contrast inserts such as muscle and change the noise texture. Its effect on leakage between material maps is not known.

3. Outcome table

Each possible answer to the Goal's question, and the action it leads to. The quantities that must not get worse are muscle contrast, CT number accuracy, noise texture, and leakage. The rows name judgments, not numeric thresholds, because several quantities trade off against an 80-fold cost, so the lab weighs the results and discusses them. The three answers lead to three different actions, so the Motivation test passes.
